04 · Experience rules

Three jobs, and abandoned carts fail at one of them.

Easy to find

Discovery by fragrance, not by SKU code

Search suggestions, fragrance and format filters, collections built around use and occasion rather than internal categories.

Easy to trust

Enough detail to decide without asking

Clear pack size and format, honest price and availability, usage and safety guidance on the page where the decision happens.

Easy to complete

A checkout that survives a weak connection

Fast mobile checkout, transparent shipping and totals, confirmation and tracking the buyer can see without calling anyone.

Most abandoned orders fail on one of these three. Almost none of them fail on price.

05 · Two visitors

A consumer and a distributor want opposite things.

Both land on the same site. One wants to buy a pack in ninety seconds. The other wants case quantities, territory terms and a person to talk to. Serving them on one path makes the site worse for both.
Consumer purchase

Buy now, in single units

  • Direct selection from collections and search
  • Fast checkout, guest or account
  • Delivery, tracking and post purchase service
  • Reorder from order history
Distributor or export enquiry

Qualify first, quote later

  • Company details and territory captured up front
  • Product interest and case quantities, not single units
  • Structured handoff to sales, never to checkout
  • No pricing exposed on the public path
Splitting the paths keeps checkout simple and keeps trade enquiries qualified.

07 · Back office

A storefront is only as good as the operation behind it.

Catalogue, stock, orders, delivery and service have to behave as one controlled flow. When they do not, the customer finds out first, and they find out after paying.

Oversell is a trust problem

Selling stock that is not there costs more than the order it wins.

Exceptions need a queue

Failed payments, cancellations and returns need somewhere to go, and somebody named.

One source of truth

Stock lives in one system. Every other view reads from it.

09 · Product model

One structure, or every system downstream inherits the mess.

A family carries the story. A variant is what somebody chooses between. An operational SKU is what the warehouse and the tax engine act on. Collapse those three and the catalogue becomes unmanageable at a hundred products, let alone a thousand.

Product family

One customer story and shared content across the range.

Sellable variant

Format, weight, pack and price. What a buyer picks.

Operational SKU

Barcode, stock, tax and fulfilment data behind each variant.

One family, three sellable variants, three operational SKUs
The launch assortment is the currently active priority SKUs. Size and export variants expand that count, and are a separate decision.
